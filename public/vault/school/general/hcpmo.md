i have so many problems with the modern institution that is the Singaporean school. and when i say this, i am talking specifically about elite JCs. and when i say this, i am specifically talking about **hwa chong institution**.

after a good six years in this godforsaken hellhole, i have seen many abhorrent things that have broken down my trust in the Singaporean school system that i once thought to be world-class. from the teachers' lacking pedagogical skills to the students' unchallenged bigotry and counsellors' appalling incompetence, this school has so many problems that i'm surprised it's still running the way it is. 
# education
## teachers
for starters, the teachers in hwa chong institution are of varying quality. sometimes you might struck gold, and your class will be assigned a teacher that is actually good at teaching. this teacher probably has some of the following qualities:
- knows how to effectively impart the subject matter to the students
- is able to capture everyone's attention
- very caring and expresses concern for students' well-being

other times, your class will be left with a teacher that probably has one of the following characteristics:
1. **very knowledgeable, but unable to teach effectively** - this is a huge problem with teachers in general, because many of them are good at the subject they are teaching and thus do not understand how to actually explain concepts to people who are weaker in the subject. ironically, these are the people who need more help, so they are just left to drown because the teacher is unable to teach them at their level of understanding, while stronger students benefit significantly more despite not needing the teacher's help as much.
2. **keeps talking about their achievements and their students' scores** - this is just annoying as fuck. i did not come here to listen to teachers yap about what previous batches of their students have accomplished, or the many books they have published. if anything, citing these things reduces their credibility since it shows that they have to rely on things other than their teaching ability to make them seem as if they are great teachers.
3. **makes everyone listen to them read off the slides** - news flash: **you are not special. i can read too.** in fact, we all had to pass several tests to prove that we could read just to get into this school. so you can imagine my frustration when my teacher makes everyone keep their devices only to force us to watch him show off his ability to read black calibri text on a white background because no one bothered to make the slides look nice. what's even worse is these teachers usually don't have much of a variation in their tone, so you just have to listen to them drone on and on without any other form of stimulation.
4. **severely lacks economy of words** - so many of these teachers' 90 minute lessons could have been emails, and i would have greatly benefitted from just reading the main points. instead, i'm forced to sit down and listen to them talk about nothing because they take so long just to convey the most basic information. ironically, the biggest offender so far has been my GP teacher.
5. **boring** - just not interesting at all. there is close to nothing keeping me engaged in these teachers' lessons and i usually end up using their lesson time to do my own work.

these problems are so common to the point where i wonder how some of these teachers even have jobs. unfortunately, the answer is probably just that there is a huge shortage of teachers in the job market, since not many people are enticed by low teacher salaries, especially when working with MOE. while i love some of my teachers (shoutout to ms tee and ms lee), they are unfortunately but a few droplets in a sea of teaching staff who are heavily underqualified.

with how ineffecient and ineffective lessons have begun to seem, it is unsurprising that so many students feel like we spend more than six hours a day in school doing absolutely nothing before going home exhausted. school has begun to seem like a huge time-waster for us, and it is up to the school to not only fix their methods but also to better accommodate the learning needs of students. i personally think that students could benefit from shorter lessons and more materials so students can spend more time studying on their own, and students who struggle to learn on their own can attend physical consultations or optional lectures. my favorite teachers have always been the ones who have just left me alone to do what i want to, because i prefer to study and learn independently. but given the decades-old model of school that still persists today, it is unlikely that such radical changes will be made anytime soon.
## departments and resources
individual teaching abilities aside, some departments are also prone to systemic complacency due to high performers. after all, elite schools are full of people who are very good at studying, and would thus be able to learn the content themselves. alternatively, many of these students can afford private tuition that makes up for the lack of teaching quality. so when all of these students score A for the subject anyway, there is no reason for the department to change anything since in the school's eyes, they are performing well.

one striking example of this is hwa chong's economics department, notorious for having terrible teachers and low-quality notes. however, people still score well because they are able to study the material independently or with external help. some departments like the computing department are teeming with talented students who already have experience in coding, meaning most of them already know a significant portion of the curriculum, which is why they are able to score even though the computing department's resources are severely lacking.

at this point, you might be wondering what exactly makes a resource good. here are several criteria i have personally come up with to determine this.
1. **organisation** - it is very important for resources like school notes to be organised properly into subtopics such that students can easily understand what exactly is expected of them in the curriculum.
2. **completeness** - resources should also contain all of the relevant information without omitting anything.
3. **clear indication of important information** - for content-heavy topics and subjects, it is very important for departments to highlight what exactly needs to be memorised so that students are not overwhelmed by the sheer amount of information they see. additionally, it just does not make sense for students to be memorising excessive amounts of information in an age where rote memorisation is becoming obsolete. thus, notes should highlight what exactly students must know for the exam so they do not waste their time and energy commiting unnecessary things to memory, which can be especially taxing for students who are not good at memorisation.

in my experience, most of hwa chong's resources are organised and complete, but what differentiates good and bad school resources is whether important information is clearly indicated. a prime example of this would be the school's h2 maths notes, where the back of every chapter's notes has a huge table containing every single individual skill that could be tested in exams. this serves as a reference for students as they now know what exactly they need to learn for the test, and they can even use this as a checklist to ensure they have an adequate understanding of the topic. on the other end of the spectrum, the h2 computing network notes contained basically nothing that helped to differentiate between what needed to be memorised for the exam, which meant students were essentially expected to memorise the whole set of notes, consisting of several powerpoint decks with over 50 pages each.

the computing department has also been, in my experience, rather poor at setting exams. a lot of the school papers i ended up doing were not of great quality, with many vague questions that were unclear about the format they expected answers in. additionally, there was a whole chapter (ethics) that was never covered properly by the teachers, and we only obtained relevant resources when i personally messaged my computing teacher to ask about it. 

this then raises several questions about how this even came to be. why doesn't the school monitor their departments even when students are doing well? do the teachers not realise that they are doing a bad job? and how long more will this endless cycle of complacency continue? unfortunately, by the time these questions can be answered, i will be too far away from the school to care.
# students
hwa chong has a very large student base. there are about 400 students per high school batch, and about 1050 students per JC batch which gives us a total of:$$4 \times 400 + 2 \times 1050 = 3700$$
because of this, everyone has their own unique experience with the schoolmates that they get to interact with over the six (or two, if you're lucky) years. 

personally, my experience with hwa chong's high school students consisted of significant amounts of bigotry, with people shouting and screaming slurs at each other like it was nothing. the n-word was repeated many times in my upper sec class, and i never understood the obsession with saying a word with so much dark history behind it. was it just to sound edgy and rebellious? i will never know nor understand.

this was also a time where i was trying to figure out how to express myself in a world where everyone around me was a straight man and i was a gay probably-not-cisgender-male. i never felt truly accepted by the people around me even though i was never really harassed by my classmates; although, when i did upload a video of myself trying to be funny onto social media, i got clowned for it and people began harassing me. i also got called my first homophobic slur, so that's great and definitely a memorable experience i will treasure for the rest of my lifetime!

even when going up to JC, i still did not have many friends for numerous reasons, including the following:
- unfounded claims and rumours about me
- me being weird
- me being gay
- me being mentally ill

i also had a problem with people being friends with me and then suddenly not wanting to be friends with me anymore, for whatever reason. when i went to hangzhou for a school trip, some of these people were literally badmouthing me behind my back.

something that i was told by my counsellor midway through JC was that people in hwa chong tend to be very fake and performative, which explains why everyone in hwa chong is so overwhelmingly normal while i'm fucking weird and gay. even among the people that i would consider to be weird and gay, i had my own issues with them, namely that many of them did not seem that interested to be friends with me, or were friends with people that i had beef with. in the end, i was mostly lonely, save for a friend group that i was able to meet up with periodically.

# counsellors
i have two words: [vincent tan](https://the-singapore-lgbt-encyclopaedia.fandom.com/wiki/Hwa_Chong_anti-gay_sex_ed_saga).

on a serious note, i personally was under vincent's care briefly when i had a spat with my parents in 2021. he called my parents to talk to them before (allegedly) calling my mother a policewoman and she was so mad that it set us back several weeks. we never spoke to him again.

i was then transferred to mr edmund teo. mr teo was incredibly sympathetic and compassionate, and i always knew i could rely on him whenever i needed to talk to someone. unfortunately, after about a year, he left for SJI, and i was caught by surprise when i logged onto google chat the next year only to see his profile picture had disappeared.

i then hopped from counsellor to counsellor, but none of the new high school counsellors were any good. they were nice, but i never really felt like anything they did was particularly helpful. none of them made me feel like i was heard, or that people cared about how i felt. for some of them, i even felt like they were just asking me questions and giving me homework for the sake of solving the problems as soon as possible, which was not what i was looking for.

when i started JC, i chose not to see any counsellors because i figured there was no one that i would want to keep seeing, and so i just gave up. however, after my hangzhou trip, i went to see ms charlene, and she defied my expectations completely. she is an ally, and she is incredibly supportive and sympathetic, making her the rare exception among all of the current hwa chong counsellors. unfortunately, most counsellors in hwa chong are not like her, but i am hoping that the school's counsellors are working toward just focusing on trying to understand students better and lending a listening ear, which is what many of today's burnt out students need above all.

# administration
i have never understood hwa chong's school administration.

## rules
i have always felt like hwa chong's school rules were very archaic and didn't make much sense. i always hated how we had to gather at 0740 on monday mornings just to have combined assembly, and that we had to have flag raising in the central plaza thrice a week despite how hot it was. the humidity has reached the point where the national anthem is accompanied by the drones of portable fans that people are sticking under their shirts to prevent sweating.

i also have so much to complain about the school's attire rules. my hair is not getting in the way of my learning, and i am able to keep it neat even when it is long. my choice of jewellery has never affected my ability to study either, but somehow female students are only allowed one pair of dark studded earrings, and male students are not allowed to have any jewellery at all. i do understand that enforcing these rules is not purely due to hwa chong's own unwillingness to let students express themselves as they have an obligation to uphold MOE's rules, but the school could still exercise much more leniency when it comes to these things.

the school's attitude towards latecoming has also been abhorrent, especially with that one time where students were given demerit points for not being in class before in-class flag-raising, which is not even a real rule. at some point, it has just begun to feel like teachers are giving out demerit points because of deep-rooted issues that they can only be temporarily relieved from if they go on a power trip.

## school leaders
anyone semi-frequently on r/sgexams has probably heard about [hwa chong's school leaders and their god awful dialogues where they fail to actually address any concerns](https://www.reddit.com/r/SGExams/comments/1t5a294/hci_school_leadership_is_a_joke/). the number of times students have begged for the issues they brought up to be addressed only for the school leaders to act dismissively is no longer countable. let's not forget mr lee peck ping and his obsession with AI, replacing our message board with a vibe-coded website that barely even works, and his blatant misogyny displayed through his dismissal of girls asking for more pockets in their skirts. i refuse to believe this man is real. and i don't even want to get started on ms deborah how and her ai slop "brain-tickling moments" posts that i unfortunately have not figured out how to automatically redirect to my spam folder. my brain does not want to be tickled by soulless machine-generated posters that are cramped with small text and ugly visuals.

another point of contention brought up in some of the dialogues and the conversation surrounding them is that the school does not seem to actually know who the classrooms belong to. obviously, from a legal perspective, the school owns the property, and by extension, the classrooms. also because of this, we are not allowed to keep anything in these classrooms since they do not belong to us. so why the hell are the classes assigned to the homerooms being blamed for any mess that occurs in them? you would think that if we are only given a certain amount of ownership of a room, we would only have to take care of it to an extent that is proportional to our ownership, but no! the j2s are the only ones responsible for the room and not the 5 other j1 classes that use it for their lessons.

also, it has always frustrated me that the school gates only open at a certain time. why don't they open any earlier? by right, if the school does not want us to play truant, wouldn't we already be punished for missing class, since that is an existing school rule? in JC, the gates only open at 1130 which i always thought was somewhat reasonable but i always questioned why they didn't open at 1030 since some students already have their break or dismissal starting then. when this issue was brought up, mr lee basically asked if people really end then or they just want to go out to eat. 

**... what is the problem with wanting to go out to eat ???**

(high school is even worse, by the way. all the signs on the gates claim that they open at 1300, which is the earliest dismissal time. however, there were many instances where i would head to the gates at 1300 only to find out that they would not open the gates until 1315 because "they did not want students to skip class to go home early", or something like that. this genuinely pissed me the fuck off, not even because the school wanted to keep us locked up for another 15 minutes. in my opinion, if the gates open at 1315, you should say they open at 1315, and not pretend they open at 1300. change the signage or something, i don't care. **but don't lie to my fucking face!**)

what i'm trying to say is that the school administration has always treated the students, a good amount of whom are already considered young adults, like children, and it pisses me off so much because it's incredibly insulting to not just me but to all of the students in this school. as much as i hate many of my schoolmates, we all deserve some respect from the school administration that we are forced to obey. but as the school leaders continue live in their ivory towers with little actual regard for students' concerns, it is unlikely that the status quo will change in the near future.

and that is why hwa chong institution pisses me the fuck off. there are genuinely not enough words to describe the pure vitriol and hatred that i feel for this disgrace of an educational institution. in ten years from now, i will probably be asked what i learnt from my time in hwa chong institution, and the only things i can really say i have learnt are that:
1. the only skills you need to become a teacher is reading off slides and bragging about your accomplishments,
2. it's okay for departments to not do anything because students will score anyway,
3. every single person in elite schools has an amazing personality and you will not get bullied ever,

may the infallible spirit of hwa chong shine forever, even as the torrential downpour of reality takes out half of the classrooms' lights.
