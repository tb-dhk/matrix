why did you choose to have children?

when you chose to have a baby, even though you were so unprepared for such a commitment that the first few years drove you into depression, did you ever wonder if when your child found out that in some way or another, he drove you to drop out of your phd programme, he would feel guilty for it?

and years later, when your children grew older, did you ever stop to think that your children might be affected by your constant outbursts? that they would eventually come to learn that loud sounds meant aggression, and aggression meant they had to be anxious and terrified? did you think that your firstborn would grow unhealthily attached to his mother because his father was no longer safe? and most of all, did it even occur to you that this would continue to give them trauma that lasted until their adolescence and potentially their adulthood?

when your firstborn started secondary school, did you not stop to think that when he failed a test for the first time in his life, the best course of action was probably not to gang up on him and call him names? when he cried for help, clawing for any form of support from strangers on the internet, did you not feel any guilt as you wrung his lifeline from his hands?

and when he had finally suffered enough and went to the school counsellor, what were you trying to achieve when you told him he was emotionally blackmailing you? what good could possibly come out of forcing your child to be responsible for your emotions by demanding he disclose them to you for your own sake, parentifying him at the age of 14? what love could possibly come out of screaming at your child when he flinched because you tried to hug him, even though you had betrayed him in so many different ways? what were you thinking when you decided to emotionally terrorise your child? were you even thinking? do you ever think?

when you talk to your friends and relatives about your children, why do you always frame it as if they were the ones to rebel? as if they decided one day that they no longer appreciated their loving parents? why do you refuse to acknowledge that deep down, you know it was your fault?

now that your firstborn has reached adulthood and has been diagnosed with mental illness, why do you try to hide his scars? when he tells you that his mental illness was caused by your abuse throughout his life, why is your first move to deflect? who are you to tell your child that his life was never that bad? or that doctors are overdiagnosing these mental conditions? or that he's experiencing the placebo effect? is it because you feel shame for your wrongdoings? or are you ashamed of your child for something that is no fault of his own?

did you know that because of you, there is a little boy stuck in the body of someone thrice his age, forced to keep living on this earth with a hole in his heart that you created? has it ever occurred to you that he didn't choose any of this, and that he had no hand in your actions? you chose to create life at a bad time. you chose to ignore his emotional needs, and you chose to let him grow up that way. now, he has to bear the brunt of your poorly made decisions.

god, if he was ever real, blessed you with a little boy with so much love to give to the world, and you chose to make sure that he learnt that no one would ever love him again. not even you would love him if you knew about all the things he was hiding from you as he grew up, because we all know you could never love a gay son. not even he could love himself because the world had taught him that he was useless and not worth it, and not only did you fail in your responsibility to prevent that, you were the main culprits.

but then again, you didn't know this would happen. because in a world full of gardeners, you would be the ones that killed all your plants because you didn't know they needed sunlight. in a world full of chefs, you would be the ones with blood on your hands because you didn't know pufferfish contain poisonous toxins. but it's okay, i guess. there really is no manual for parenting, and so it's not your fault. you just didn't know any of the things that it was your responsibility to know.

now, your child is forced to live out the rest of his days feeling completely alone, with an illness that, as far as he knows, is incurable and terminal. i hope you're happy.
